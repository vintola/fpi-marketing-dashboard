Freedom Property Investors · Marketing & conversion analysisExecutive summary
The short version

It is a trust business with a lead-generation front end.

Freedom does not sell a commodity online. It sells a high-consideration advisory relationship, backed by research, access and an end-to-end team. The website creates belief; the masterclass creates intent; the sales conversation converts intent into a member. That distinction changes what marketing should optimise.

01 · THE ENGINE

The masterclass is the hinge, not the website.

The site is a handover, not a closer. Paid attention is converted into a free masterclass registration, then into attendance, then into a one-to-one strategy conversation. The real revenue event happens after the website has done its job.

02 · THE LEAK

The biggest losses are after registration.

Registration is the easy part. Attendance, consultation booking, show-up and close are where high-ticket funnels usually break. A 10-point attendance gain can lift members by roughly 25% at the same media spend — and cut CAC by about 20%.

03 · THE CONSTRAINT

Last-click ROAS is not safe for budget decisions.

The purchase is high-ticket, multi-touch and often closed offline. Platform ROAS over-credits retargeting and brand search, under-credits upper-funnel video, and cannot see the member. Without CRM-to-platform feedback, media buys the wrong event.

04 · THE FRICTION

Proof is strong but inconsistent.

Public pages cite 1,600+, 2,000+, 2,200+ and 2,600+ Google reviews, and live Lorem Ipsum still appears in key sections. In a trust purchase, inconsistency is not cosmetic; it is a conversion tax on every paid click.

Recommended North Star

Incremental New Members at Target CAC

One growth number — net new paying members, measured incrementally — governed by three guardrails: a fully loaded CAC ceiling derived from LTV, a 90-day retention/refund check, and incremental ROAS.

Count what pays. Measure what caused it. Protect the economics.

What I would do first

  • Close the measurement loop. Push HubSpot member events back to Meta and Google as offline conversions.
  • Fix proof and trust. One review count, no placeholder copy, one clear hero promise.
  • Treat the masterclass as a product. Registration, reminders, attendance, replay and booking are separate conversion problems.
  • Set a CAC ceiling from LTV and payback. Do not let platform ROAS set the budget.
Analyst take. The opportunity is not to spend more. It is to convert more of the attention the brand already earns, and to prove which attention creates members. That is a measurement and middle-of-funnel problem before it is a media-buying problem.

The revenue equation

Revenue = Reach × Registration × Attendance × MQL × Booking × Show × Close × First-year value × Retention

Every marketing decision should be judged by which term it moves — and whether it moves that term profitably. Most reporting stops at “registration”, which is only the second term.

The website’s real job

It creates enough belief and clarity for a qualified prospect to trade their time for a masterclass or a call. It is a handover to a human sales process, not the place where the sale closes. That means the right website metrics are registration rate, booking rate and show rate — not “add to cart”.

The masterclass’s real job

It converts attention into intent by combining education, authority, proof and a next step. The offer bundles four promises — tax reduction, mortgage freedom, boom locations and early retirement — which broadens appeal but also increases the need for qualification.

Three implications

  • Do not optimise to the lead. A cheap registration that never attends is a cost, not an asset.
  • Do not judge the website on revenue. Judge it on qualified handoffs; judge the sales team on close.
  • Do not scale media without sales capacity. The constraint moves from clicks to consultations to closers as spend rises.

Why LTV matters more than AOV

In a high-ticket advisory, first-year revenue is not the right ceiling for CAC. If a member buys again, refers, or attaches finance and tax services, the lifetime contribution can be multiples of the first fee. The business can rationally pay more than first-year revenue to acquire the right member.

But that logic only holds if retention, refunds and attach rates are measured. Otherwise a high LTV assumption becomes a licence to overspend.

The analyst’s read. Freedom’s model can support a premium CAC, but only if the business knows three things it does not publish: contribution margin per member, cohort retention, and the true cost of the sales organisation. Those three numbers — not the ad-platform ROAS — should set the acquisition budget.

04 · Challenges

Attention is not the scarce resource. Trust is.

The category is crowded, expensive and sceptical. The marketing challenge is to make a high-ticket promise credible at scale, without looking like the noise it competes with.

01

Trust at scale in a sceptical category

Property investment has a long tail of aggressive education and get-rich-quick operators. Buyers arrive with defences up. Generic promises about “boom locations” or “tax to zero” can trigger scepticism rather than desire.

Countermeasure: specific, verifiable proof; named experts; transparent process; conservative claims; strong disclaimers.
02

A commoditised masterclass offer

“Free masterclass + boom locations + tax reduction” is a widely copied format. The offer is strong, but not unique. Differentiation has to come from the quality of the research, the calibre of the live experts and the specificity of the outcomes.

Countermeasure: lead with proprietary data and a live expert experience, not the generic promise.
03

Rising paid costs and creative fatigue

Meta and YouTube costs rise with competition and frequency. Founder-led video can fatigue quickly; the same face and the same four promises lose stopping power. Creative is a variable cost, not a one-off asset.

Countermeasure: a creative factory with founder, data, member-story and expert formats; refresh before CAC moves.
04

Founder and key-person dependency

Scott and Lianna are the brand’s credibility. That is an asset, but also a concentration risk: scaling spend without scaling the faces and voices of the brand limits reach and resilience.

Countermeasure: build a bench of named experts and member advocates; diversify formats and channels.
05

Multi-segment complexity

First-time investors, experienced investors and retirees have different motivations, objections, risk tolerance, price sensitivity and lifetime value. One hero message and one nurture sequence under-serve all three.

Countermeasure: segment-specific offers, proof and sales playbooks; measure CAC and LTV by segment.
06

Regulatory and platform risk

Financial and property claims, testimonials, “#1” positioning and performance examples attract scrutiny. Meta and Google also impose verification, targeting and creative restrictions on financial products and services.

Countermeasure: a substantiation file, legal review, compliant creative templates and a single source of truth for claims.

Proof is a conversion asset — and it is inconsistent

Quick wins vs strategic bets

Quick wins (weeks): proof consistency, remove placeholder copy, reminder sequence, five-minute lead SLA, creative refresh.

Strategic bets (quarters): closed-loop measurement, segment journeys, predictive scoring, incrementality testing, sales capacity model.

06 · The North Star

Incremental New Members at Target CAC.

A North Star should do three things: align marketing, sales and finance; resist gaming; and connect daily work to economic value. Lead volume fails all three. Platform ROAS fails the second and third.

Recommended company North Star

Incremental New Members at Target CAC (INM)

Net new paying members in the period, attributable to incremental marketing activity, where fully loaded CAC is at or below the ceiling implied by lifetime value and payback.

Why this one

  • It counts what pays. Members are the first revenue event, not registrations or clicks.
  • It demands causality. “Incremental” forces holdouts or lift tests, so brand and retargeting cannot simply take credit for demand that already existed.
  • It protects economics. A member acquired above the CAC ceiling is growth that destroys value, so it does not count.
  • It connects functions. Marketing creates the member, sales closes the member, finance defines the value, data proves the causality.

Options considered — and rejected

MetricWhy not the North Star
Leads / registrationsEasy to grow, easy to game, silent on quality and revenue.
CPLCheap leads can be the most expensive members.
Platform ROASAttribution-biased, not incremental, ignores payback and retention.
CAC aloneCan be improved by shrinking spend and starving growth.
Members aloneCan be bought at any price; no economic discipline.
Member Lifetime ContributionThe right long-term finance North Star, but too slow for weekly marketing operation.

The CAC ceiling

Set the maximum fully loaded CAC from contribution, not revenue:

CAC ceiling = (First-year contribution × LTV multiple) ÷ Target LTV:CAC

Guardrails, not afterthoughts.

Count a member toward INM only if: fully loaded CAC ≤ ceiling; the member is retained for 90 days and not refunded; and the acquisition is supported by incremental evidence (holdout, lift test or a credible attribution model).

Operating cadence

Weekly · act

Monthly · allocate

CAC by channel and segment, LTV:CAC, payback, lead-to-member, refunds, budget reallocation, sales capacity. Owner: Growth + Finance + Sales.

Quarterly · prove

Geo holdouts, conversion lift, cohort LTV, MMM, brand lift, compliance review, annual plan. Owner: Data + Growth + Finance.

Leading versus lagging

Lead weekly: registration, attendance, speed-to-lead, booking. Lead monthly: CAC, LTV:CAC, payback. Prove quarterly: incremental ROAS.

The 30-minute weekly review

1) North Star and guardrails. 2) Funnel variance by stage. 3) Creative and channel. 4) Sales capacity. 5) Decisions and owners.

The test of the operating model. Every review should end with a decision, an owner and a date. Otherwise it is a reporting ritual, not a growth system.

08 · The measurement spine

Build the measurement before you scale the spend.

The platforms will always report a version of success. The business needs one reconciled view from ad click to member, with offline outcomes fed back to the platforms that created them.

Ad platformsMeta · Google · YouTube
→
Landing pagesGA4 · UTMs · consent
→
HubSpotLead lifecycle · masterclass
→
Sales & callsBooked · held · closed
→
FinanceRevenue · refunds · LTV
→
WarehouseDashboard · cohorts
Close the loop. Send member and refund events back to Meta (Conversions API) and Google (enhanced conversions) as offline conversions. Without this, the platforms optimise to form fills and the business optimises to the wrong number.

Minimum viable stack

  • Server-side Google Tag Manager and consent-safe tracking.
  • One UTM taxonomy and a channel-mapping dictionary.
  • HubSpot lifecycle stages: lead → MQL → consultation booked → held → member → retained/refunded.
  • Call tracking with dispositions, not just duration.
  • Offline conversion uploads and a monthly match-rate check.
  • Cohort LTV and CAC payback by channel and segment.
  • Quarterly geo holdouts or conversion-lift tests; MMM only after clean data.
  • Data-quality tests and a monthly reconciliation to finance.

90-day sequence

Days 1–30

Tracking audit; define MQL and member; CAPI and enhanced conversions; site trust fixes; masterclass reminder/replay; five-minute SLA.

Proof: member events visible in platforms; attendance and speed-to-lead baselines.

Days 31–60

Segment-specific journeys; creative sprint; reallocate toward efficient channels; lead-scoring v1; weekly dashboard.

Proof: CAC by segment; creative winners; lead-quality lift.

Days 61–90

First geo holdout; cohort LTV; sales capacity model; quarterly planning; compliance review.

Proof: incremental ROAS; payback by cohort; defensible budget decision.

What to stop

Reporting lead volume without quality. Optimising to CPL. Letting platform ROAS set budget. Scaling spend without sales capacity. Running “#1” and performance claims without a substantiation file.

What to start

Offline conversion feedback. Weekly funnel review. Segment-level CAC and LTV. Creative refresh cadence. Quarterly incrementality. A single source of truth for proof.

What to prove

That paid media creates incremental members, not just incremental clicks. That the masterclass creates pipeline, not just registrations. That higher spend still clears the CAC ceiling.

09 · What I would do next

Ten moves, in priority order.

These are ordered by dependency and impact. Measurement comes first because every later decision depends on it; trust comes second because it taxes every acquisition dollar.

1

Make the member the unit of measurement

Move reporting from leads to members, and from platform ROAS to fully loaded CAC and payback.

2

Close the offline loop

Push member and refund events back to Meta and Google so media optimises to the right event.

3

Set a CAC ceiling from LTV

Derive the maximum fully loaded CAC from contribution margin, LTV multiple and target LTV:CAC.

4

Treat the masterclass as a product

Manage registration, reminders, attendance, replay and booking as separate conversion problems.

5

Fix proof and trust inconsistencies

One verified review count, no Lorem Ipsum, one hero promise, and a substantiation file for every claim.

6

Build a creative factory, not a campaign

Founder-led authority, data explainers, member stories and expert voices, refreshed on a cadence.

7

Segment the funnel and the scorecard

Different offers, proof, nurture and sales playbooks for first-time, experienced and retiree investors.

8

Make speed-to-lead non-negotiable

A five-minute SLA, round-robin routing, instant booking and escalation alerts.

9

Align sales capacity with media spend

Model consultations per week and closers required. Scaling past capacity turns budget into no-shows.

10

Diversify beyond paid social

Search captures intent; partnerships, referrals, reviews and organic content lower blended CAC.

What I would not do.

Scale on platform ROAS. Judge YouTube on last-click CAC. Optimise to CPL. Buy leads without sales capacity. Repeat unsubstantiated claims. Build MMM before the CRM data is clean.

Measurement

Member events in platforms; match rate ≥80%; one reconciled CAC.

Conversion

Attendance ≥55%; 80% of leads contacted in 5 minutes; lead-to-member ≥3%.

Economics

Blended CAC -8% to -12%; payback ≤6 months; first incremental read.

The bottom line

Freedom already has the hard part: a differentiated offer, credible expertise and a market that wants the outcome. The next stage is not more attention — it is better conversion of the attention it earns, and the measurement to prove which attention creates members.

Analytical assumptions

  • Membership pricing, revenue mix, CAC, conversion rates, retention and sales cost are not public. Any numbers in this document are illustrative models, not company results.
  • The funnel order and revenue streams are inferred from the public customer journey, service descriptions and brand assets.
  • “Fully loaded CAC” includes media, sales, marketing operations, content and tools. “Media CAC” is media-only and understates the true cost.
  • LTV should be contribution-based, after delivery, refunds and servicing. The CAC ceiling examples use contribution before those adjustments and are therefore optimistic.
  • Incremental ROAS requires a holdout, lift test or credible causal model. Platform-reported ROAS is not incremental ROAS.

Glossary

CAC: cost to acquire one paying member, fully loaded.

LTV: contribution expected from a member over the relationship.

MER: revenue per dollar of total media spend.

iROAS: incremental revenue divided by spend, measured against a holdout.

MQL: a lead that meets the agreed fit and intent criteria.

Offline conversion: a CRM event (member, refund) sent back to an ad platform.

Geo holdout: withholding spend in matched regions to measure incremental effect.
